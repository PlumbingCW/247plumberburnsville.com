Repiping in Burnsville, MN.

Repiping replaces worn supply lines with new copper or PEX. We help Burnsville homeowners decide whether repeated leaks, discolored water or low pressure justify a full or partial repipe.

Repiping is the replacement of the water supply lines in a house. It is not the same as sewer or drain work. The pipes that carry clean water to your fixtures can corrode, scale up or fail at joints after decades of use, and when they do, repair becomes a repeating cost.

The decision is rarely urgent, but it should not be put off if pipes keep failing. A burst line floods a house in hours, and our burst pipe repair page covers that emergency. Repiping is the planned way to avoid it.

Signs a house may need repiping.

Repeated leaks.

Two or three leaks in different places within a few years suggest the whole run is failing.

Rusty or brown water.

Water that runs brown when it sits suggests corrosion inside galvanized or older pipe.

Low pressure.

Scale narrows pipes over time and reduces flow at every fixture.

Pinhole leaks in copper.

Tiny blue-green stains on copper are a sign of failure from the inside.

Hard water and pipe life.

The City of Burnsville's 2025 report lists hardness at 25 to 26 grains per gallon. Hard water deposits scale inside pipes and fittings, which reduces flow and can accelerate wear at joints. A softener helps, and it is worth discussing before the new pipe goes in so the whole system is planned together.

The city's report also lists 17,004 non-lead service lines and none requiring replacement for lead or galvanized steel. That covers the city side of the connection. The pipes inside your house are your own and vary by age. Our water line repair page covers the line from the meter to the house.

How a repipe works.

Survey.

We inspect the existing pipe, pressure and fixtures and walk the house with you.

Plan.

You get a written scope, the material and where walls or ceilings need opening.

Install.

New lines are run, fixtures are reconnected and old lines are removed or capped.

Test and patch.

We pressure-test the system and arrange the access repairs.

Partial repipes are common. If the leaks are in one section, such as a bathroom run or the water heater connection, replacing only that section may be the practical choice. We say plainly what a partial repair will and will not solve.

Permits and licensing.

The City of Burnsville requires a plumbing permit for many plumbing projects, and Minnesota requires plumbing work to be done by licensed plumbers or by the homeowner who owns and occupies the property. Check any plumber with the Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry's Check a License tool, and ask who pulls the permit before you sign.

A repipe is also a good moment to upgrade shutoff valves and fixtures, or to plan new fixtures through plumbing installation.

The standard for supply pipe.

Copper, PEX and CPVC are all materials the Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, addresses for water supply. We explain which one fits your house and why, and the permit step makes sure the finished work is inspected.
