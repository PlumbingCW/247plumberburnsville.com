Sump pump failures are dispatched same day, including nights and weekends, because water is rising while you wait.

A sump pump sits in a pit in the lowest part of the basement and pushes groundwater out through a discharge pipe before it reaches the floor. When it stops, the pit fills and the water spreads. Most failures come from a few causes, and most can be solved in one visit.

Minnesota thaws and heavy rain are the usual triggers. Frozen ground sheds meltwater quickly, and a pump that has sat idle all winter often fails on its first real test.

Symptom.Likely cause.Typical fix.
Pump hums but does not move water.Jammed impeller or clogged intake.Clean the intake or replace the pump.
Pump never turns on.Stuck float switch or dead motor.Replace the switch or the pump.
Pump runs constantly.Stuck float or failed check valve.Reset the float, replace the check valve.
Pump cycles on and off rapidly.Check valve missing or failed, pit too small.Install or replace the check valve.
Loud rattling or grinding.Worn bearings or debris in the pump.Replace the pump.

Pump failure is predictable.

Pumps wear out. A typical pump does not last forever, and one that has run through many seasons is a candidate for replacement before it fails. Test yours twice a year by pouring a bucket of water into the pit and watching the float rise and the pump clear it.

A battery backup is worth discussing. A storm that knocks out power also stops the main pump, and that is the moment the basement needs it most. A backup pump runs on a battery for a limited time and is a separate unit from the main pump.

Where the water goes.

The discharge pipe should carry water away from the foundation, and in winter it must not freeze at the outlet. Water that returns to the house or drains onto a neighbor's property creates a new problem. We check where the pipe exits and whether it has a proper slope and screen.

Check with the City of Burnsville about local rules on sump discharge, including whether it may connect to the sanitary sewer. Building Inspections at 952-895-4444 can answer. If a basement has repeated water problems even with a working pump, see basement waterproofing for what else can be done.

Signs you may need a new pump.

  • The pump is more than ten years old.
  • It runs often even in dry weather.
  • It makes new noises, such as grinding or rattling.
  • The pit has standing water that does not drain.
  • It has never been tested since it was installed.

A wet basement is sometimes a plumbing leak, not groundwater. Our leak detection page explains how to tell, and a stuck check valve or a damaged discharge line may be a burst pipe problem on the discharge side.

Discharge standards.

Where a sump pump may discharge is a local rule as well as a plumbing code matter under Minnesota Rules chapter 4714. Ask Burnsville Building Inspections before connecting a sump line to any sewer.
