A failed water heater: what to do now.

Water pooling around a tank usually means the tank wall has failed, and that is the one water heater problem repair cannot solve. Shut off the cold supply and the power or gas, then call. We answer 24/7, and same-day replacement is the usual plan when a tank is leaking.

You do not need to decide on the brand during the call. We start by confirming the fuel type, tank size and vent arrangement you already have, and give you the available choices that fit those.

Choosing the replacement.

Same fuel, same size.

The fastest swap. Connections line up and permit work is simple.

Larger tank.

Worth it if the household has grown or showers run cold, but the space must fit it.

Tankless.

Frees floor space and does not run out, but needs gas and vent capacity and a different budget.

Heat pump unit.

Uses less electricity, but needs room, airflow and a condensate drain.

Gas and electric tanks remain the most common replacements in Burnsville basements. A few homeowners ask about moving to tankless or heat pump models. If you want to compare, our tankless water heater page covers the practical differences, and installing a unit where none exists is handled on water heater installation.

What a replacement visit includes.

Shut down.

Water, power or gas are turned off and the old tank is drained.

Remove.

The old unit is disconnected and hauled out of the house.

Install.

New connections, a new relief valve and discharge pipe, expansion control where required and venting for gas units.

Test.

We fill the tank, purge air, light or energize the unit and verify temperature and leaks.

Parts that should be replaced with the tank.

  • Flexible water connectors, if they are corroded or older than the tank.
  • The temperature and pressure relief valve and its discharge pipe.
  • Shutoff valves that no longer close fully.
  • Gas flex connector and dirt leg on gas units.
  • Vent connections that show rust or poor slope.

Hard water changes the math.

Burnsville's 2025 water quality report gives hardness as 25 to 26 grains per gallon. That is hard enough to coat a heater element or fill a tank bottom with scale in a few years. A new tank starts clean, but without a softener or a regular flush it will gather scale the same way.

Ask about an anode rod check at each service visit and a yearly flush. Both are inexpensive next to the cost of replacing a tank early. The softener discussion lives on the water softener page.

Permits, venting and the city.

The City of Burnsville lists water heaters and water softeners among the projects handled through its plumbing permit process, which runs through the Building Inspections Department at 952-895-4444. A licensed plumber pulls the permit for the work. A homeowner who owns and occupies the property may also apply, according to the city. For gas units the vent connection, combustion air and a gas line that meets code matter as much as the tank itself.

Minnesota licenses plumbers and gives homeowners a way to verify any contractor: the Department of Labor and Industry's Check a License tool. Use it before you sign for work.

The standard behind the swap.

A replacement tank is installed to the Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714. That standard covers the relief valve and its discharge pipe, the vent on gas units and the shutoff valves, which is why we replace those parts rather than reuse worn ones.
