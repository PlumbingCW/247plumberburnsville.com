Water Heater Installation in Burnsville, MN.

Installing a water heater in a new space or changing fuel types is planned work. We confirm gas, electric and venting requirements first so the unit is placed where it can run safely and service easily.

Water heater installation covers the jobs where there is no failing tank to swap: a finished basement bath that needs a second heater, a remodel that moves the utility room, a house converting from electric to gas or a new build. The work starts on paper, with questions about fuel, space, drain, venting and demand.

If your current tank has failed and you need hot water today, see water heater replacement instead. That page covers the emergency-first process.

What we plan before an installation.

Fuel and supply.

Natural gas line size, electric panel capacity and the circuit run to the heater.

Venting.

Gas heaters need a code-compliant flue or direct vent path.

Drain and pan.

The relief valve discharge and a drain pan with a drain path protect the floor.

Space and access.

Clearances for service and for removing the unit later.

Common Burnsville installation projects.

A finished basement with a bathroom or wet bar sometimes justifies a dedicated heater. A remodeled kitchen or a new bath addition can overload one tank. A smaller secondary tank near a far bath cuts the wait for hot water.

Any change to fuel type, venting or location is worth checking with the city first. The City of Burnsville lists water heaters and softeners among the projects handled by its Building Inspections Department at 952-895-4444.

The installation steps.

Survey.

We confirm location, fuel, venting and the code requirements for the space.

Rough-in.

Supply lines, gas or electric runs, venting and drain are installed.

Set and connect.

The heater is set level, connected and fitted with safeties.

Inspect and start.

Permit inspection is arranged, then the unit is fired or energized and tested.

Water quality matters before you commission a heater.

The City of Burnsville's 2025 report puts tap water hardness at 25 to 26 grains per gallon. A new heater in a hard water house scales up unless the water is treated or flushed. When we install a heater we raise the softener question early, because the best time to add a softener is when the plumbing is already open.

See water softener installation for the details. For a tank that needs ongoing attention, our water heater repair visits include sediment and anode checks.

Licensing and inspection.

Minnesota requires plumbing work to be done by licensed plumbers, or by the homeowner who owns and occupies the home under Burnsville's permit rules. You can verify any plumber with the Check a License tool from the Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry. Permits for a new heater run through the City of Burnsville's online permit portal.

Code requirements for a new heater.

The Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, is the standard for where a heater may sit and how it is vented, drained and supplied. Ask any installer which parts of the code apply to your space before the work starts.
