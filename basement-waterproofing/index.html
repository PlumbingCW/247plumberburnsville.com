Basement Waterproofing in Burnsville, MN.

A basement that stays damp or floods after each hard rain needs more than a pump. We look at drainage, the sump system and how water reaches the foundation, then explain the options.

Waterproofing is the work of keeping groundwater out of a basement, and it starts with finding out where the water comes from. It might be a leaking plumbing line, a failed sump, a window well that fills, a crack in the foundation or surface water that drains toward the house.

A plumber is the right call when the fix involves drains, a sump system or pipes. Cracks in walls and exterior regrading may need a different contractor, and we say so when that is true.

A damp basement after one rare storm is different from a basement that is wet every spring. The soil type on your lot and the height of the water table affect how quickly water drains. Frozen ground in late winter keeps meltwater on the surface, where it finds the foundation.

We ask when the water appears, how much, and what it looks like. That tells us more than a general inspection.

Interior drainage and the sump.

An interior drain system collects water at the footing and sends it to a sump pit, where a pump discharges it outside. It is the plumbing-side answer to groundwater pressure and works when water is reaching the basement along the floor edge.

The sump is the heart of the system, so its condition matters. See sump pump repair for pump failures and backups. A system with a new pump and a battery backup protects a finished basement far better than one that depends on a pump of unknown age.

What a plumber can and cannot fix.

Plumber work.

Drain tile, sump pits and pumps, discharge lines, floor drains, backwater valves and supply leaks.

Other trades.

Structural foundation cracks, wall sealing and exterior excavation or regrading.

Homeowner steps.

Extend downspouts, clean gutters and slope soil away from the foundation.

Together.

Many wet basements need both a drainage fix and a surface fix.

Before you finish a basement.

If you plan to finish or add a bath to a basement, fix the water first. New flooring and walls on top of a damp slab fail early. A basement bathroom also needs a plan for waste, which is covered on plumbing installation.

Some basements take on water because a sewer line is blocked and the floor drain is the lowest opening. If the water is dirty or smells, it is sewage. See sewer line repair.

Where plumbing code applies.

Drain tile, sump pits and discharge lines are plumbing work under the Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714. Wall sealing and grading are outside that standard and belong to another trade.

Questions about basement waterproofing.

A plumber handles drains, sump systems, pumps and plumbing leaks. Foundation cracks and exterior regrading may need another trade, and we say so when that applies.

Frozen ground keeps meltwater on the surface, so it flows toward the foundation. A high water table and a weak sump make it worse.

Check the water meter with all fixtures off. If the meter moves, there is a plumbing leak. If not, the water is likely groundwater.

If your basement is finished or has flooded before, a backup is a good addition. A storm that knocks out power also stops the main pump.

We do plumbing work. We can tell you what surface work would help and recommend you find a landscaping or exterior contractor for that.
