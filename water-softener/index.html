Water Softener Installation in Burnsville, MN.

Burnsville tap water is very hard, and scale shows up in fixtures, water heaters and appliances. We install, repair and set up water softeners so the hardness your house sees matches the system.

A water softener removes calcium and magnesium from the water before it reaches your pipes and fixtures. In a hard water area it is one of the most useful additions to a home, and one of the most commonly misconfigured.

The City of Burnsville's 2025 drinking water report lists hardness at 25 to 26 grains per gallon. Water above about 10.5 grains per gallon is classed as very hard, so Burnsville tap water is well into that range.

What hard water does to a house.

Water heaters.

Scale builds on the tank bottom and electric elements, which shortens life and raises operating cost.

Fixtures and faucets.

Chalky deposits clog aerators and shower heads and wear seals.

Appliances.

Dishwashers, washers and ice makers scale up over time.

Soap and laundry.

Soap does not lather well and leaves residue on skin and fabric.

Each of these shows up in a separate repair page: water heater repair, faucet repair and tankless water heater service all mention scale as a leading cause of failure here. A softener is the root fix.

How softeners work.

A softener holds resin beads that trap hardness minerals. When the beads fill, the unit regenerates by flushing them with salt brine and sends the minerals down the drain. The settings tell it when to regenerate, and those settings should match your actual hardness and your household's water use.

The City of Burnsville's permit page lists water softeners among the projects that are permitted. Brine discharge goes to the drain with an air gap, and the unit needs a nearby outlet and a bypass valve. A licensed plumber handles the connection.

Repair and maintenance.

Softeners fail in a few common ways. A salt bridge forms and stops the brine from dissolving. The resin wears out after many years. A valve or control head fails, or the drain line clogs.

We can diagnose and repair most units. We also help owners who moved into a home with an old softener and do not know how it is set. A system set for the wrong hardness wastes salt or leaves hardness in the water.

Questions to ask about softener salt and drainage.

Softeners discharge brine to the drain during regeneration. Ask about local rules before you connect, since some communities restrict softener brine in sensitive areas. Building Inspections at 952-895-4444 can confirm what applies in Burnsville.

Drain and backflow standards.

A softener must discharge to a drain with an air gap and be installed to the Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714. That standard is the reason the brine line never connects directly to the drain.
