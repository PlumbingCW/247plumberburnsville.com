Emergency Water Line Repair in Burnsville, MN.

The water service line carries water from the city main to your house. When it leaks or breaks you may see a soggy yard, lose pressure or watch the meter spin. We trace, repair and replace service lines, and answer 24/7.

Water line emergency: signs to act on now.

A line break between the meter and the house is an emergency when water is surfacing or the house has lost supply. Close the main valve inside the house and call. We answer 24/7 and dispatch these calls same day. If the break is on the city side of the meter, call Burnsville Public Works as well.

Many service line failures are slow. A wet strip in the lawn, a patch of unusually green grass, a drop in pressure or a high bill with no explanation are the usual hints.

Wet or soft ground.

Water surfacing along the line between the street and the house.

Falling pressure.

Pressure that drops across the whole house suggests a leak before the branches.

Meter keeps moving.

The meter turns after the main valve is closed, so the leak is outside the house.

Dirty or discolored water.

A break can let soil into the line.

Who owns what.

The City of Burnsville's Water and Sewer Maintenance page says every home and business has a water meter, usually near the laundry room or furnace. It does not say where the city's responsibility for the line ends, so call Public Works at 952-895-4550 before you dig or pay for a repair.

The city's 2025 drinking water report also publishes a service line inventory. It lists 17,004 non-lead service lines and none requiring replacement for lead or galvanized steel. That tells you about the pipe material, not about where a leak sits, so the meter test and a locator still do the real work.

Confirm the leak.

We check the meter with the house shut off and test pressure.

Locate.

Acoustic equipment and a pipe locator trace the line and mark the leak.

Repair or replace.

We dig to the break and replace the section, or propose a longer replacement if the pipe is failing along its length.

Test and restore.

The line is pressure tested before backfill and the area is restored.

Frost depth and Minnesota service lines.

Minnesota lines must run below the frost line, and a line that is too shallow freezes in a hard winter. If your line froze or you lost water in a cold snap, ask us to check the depth when we are on site. Our burst pipe repair page covers frozen lines inside the house.

A leak in the service line can also look like a leak elsewhere. Leak detection separates the two before any digging starts.

Before we dig.

Minnesota requires utility locates before excavation. Gopher State One Call marks buried utilities at no cost to the property owner, and we arrange the locate before any digging begins.

Depth and materials on the service line.

Service line materials and installation follow the standard in the Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714. The code also expects lines to be protected from frost, which is why we check burial depth when the trench is open.

Pipes inside the house are a different job from the buried service line. If the supply pipes in the walls are old or leaking in several places, repiping covers replacing them.
