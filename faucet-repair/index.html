Emergency Faucet Repair in Burnsville, MN.

A steady drip wastes water, and a faucet that will not shut off can flood a kitchen. We repair or replace faucets, cartridges, valves and supply lines, and take emergency calls 24/7.

A faucet that sprays under the sink or will not shut off is an emergency. Turn the small shutoff valves under the sink clockwise to stop the water. If they do not close, shut off the main. We answer 24/7 and dispatch these calls same day.

A drip is not an emergency, but it is not nothing. A faucet dripping once a second can waste thousands of gallons a year, and the cause only gets worse with time.

Hard water takes a toll on faucets.

The City of Burnsville's 2025 water report lists hardness at 25 to 26 grains per gallon. Hard water leaves chalky deposits that clog aerators, stiffen handles and wear seals. A faucet in Burnsville often fails from scale before it fails from age.

Unscrewing the aerator and soaking it in vinegar helps with low flow. If the handle is hard to turn or the faucet leaks around the base, the cartridge is likely scaled. A water softener reduces this wear for every faucet in the house.

Repair or replace the faucet.

Repair is usually the right choice when the faucet is in good shape and the part is available. Replacement makes sense when the finish is worn, the faucet is a discontinued model or repairs would cost close to a new one.

If you are adding a faucet in a remodel, that is plumbing installation work. We can also replace the supply lines and shutoff valves at the same time, which prevents a second call when an old valve fails.

Drip from the spout.

Usually a worn washer or cartridge.

Water at the base.

Failed O-rings or a loose mounting nut.

Low pressure.

A clogged aerator or a scaled cartridge.

Water under the sink.

A failed supply line, compression nut or drain connection.

Check under the sink.

Many faucet calls turn out to be a slow leak at a supply line or drain fitting rather than the faucet itself. A dry paper towel held to each connection shows moisture quickly. If the cabinet floor is soft or stained, tell us when you call so we can check for damage.

A slow drip under the sink is also one of the places a small leak starts. If you are not sure where water is coming from, leak detection can pin it down.

Backflow and supply standards.

Faucet supply connections and shutoff valves follow the Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714. Replacing worn shutoff valves during a faucet repair keeps the connection to that standard.
