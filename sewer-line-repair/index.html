Sewer backup: what to do first.

Do these while we are on the way.

  • Stop using water everywhere in the house, including the washer and dishwasher..
  • Keep people and pets out of any area with sewage, and do not touch it with bare hands..
  • Turn off power to a flooded basement only if you can reach the panel without standing in water..
  • Take photos of the water line and damaged items for your insurer..

We handle sewer line emergencies at night and on weekends. If the problem is in the city main rather than your line, Burnsville directs residents to call Public Works on weekdays and 9-1-1 evenings and weekends.

A sewer line fails slowly or all at once. Slow failures show up as drains that back up every few months, a damp patch in the lawn, or a smell near the foundation. Sudden failures happen when a section collapses or a root mass seals the pipe entirely, and the result is a backup in the lowest fixture within hours.

Repair starts with finding the failure, not with digging. A camera on a push cable travels the pipe and shows the exact spot, the type of damage and how far it is from the house or the street. That means a spot repair of a short section instead of an open trench across the yard.

Repair options, with honest trade-offs.

Option.Good fit.Trade-off.
Spot repair (cut out and replace).One short damaged section, easy to reach.Needs a trench over that section.

Cleaning only.Blockage with healthy pipe.Does not fix a defect.

Lining is only one tool and not a default. It works when the host pipe still holds its shape. If a section has dropped or pulled apart, there is nothing to line, and replacement is the honest answer. We use the camera footage to explain the reasoning so you can see it yourself.

Signs the line itself is failing.

Repeat backups.

Back-to-back clogs after professional cleaning point to a defect in the pipe.

Soggy yard patch.

A wet strip over the lateral during dry weather suggests a leak below grade.

Sewer smell outside.

Gas escaping through a crack often reaches the surface near the foundation.

Slow drains in the lowest fixture only.

Water pools in a sag in the pipe and drains slowly, then backs up when the line fills.

What we see in Burnsville lateral lines.

Burnsville's housing boom came in the 1970s, and sewer laterals from that period may be cast iron, clay tile or early plastic, depending on the builder.

Each fails differently. Cast iron scales and then cracks along its length. Clay develops open joints that roots find. Early plastic can sag where the trench backfill settled.

Mature trees near an older lateral put roots in direct reach of those joints. Newer pipe usually has tighter joints, so root intrusion is often less advanced there. The camera tells us which story your lot has.

Permits and licensing.

Plumbing permits in Burnsville are handled through the Building Inspections Department at 952-895-4444, and the city says a permit is required when work breaks the seal on the drain system or runs new pipelines. Minnesota also licenses plumbers. You can verify any plumber with the Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry's Check a License tool before work begins.

If a failed sewer line sits near the street, the right fix may involve more than one party. We tell you what belongs to you and what does not. For a line where the cause is only a blockage, see our drain cleaning page. For planned inspections before a purchase, see sewer inspection.

Materials and permits for a repair.

Replacement sewer pipe must be a material the state standard, the Minnesota Plumbing Code in Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, allows, and repairs in the ground are usually inspected. Ask who pulls the permit before the work is scheduled.

A backup that has just started is usually cleared before any repair is scoped. Sewer line cleaning is the first step in most of these calls, and the camera work that follows shows whether a repair is needed.
