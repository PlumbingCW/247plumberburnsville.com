First ten minutes
.

What to do before the plumber arrives.

The first minutes of a plumbing emergency decide how much damage there is to clean up. These steps apply to most calls.

Stop the water.

Close the main valve, usually near the meter by the laundry room or furnace.

Stop using drains.

If a drain is backing up, keep the washer, dishwasher and sinks off.

Protect the room.

Move rugs, boxes and electronics off wet floors and away from the leak.

Call and describe.

Tell the dispatcher what you see and hear so the right tools come along.

Specific advice for specific problems is on the service pages. A leaking water heater, a frozen pipe and a toilet that overflows all have different first steps.

Local plumbing
.

Why Burnsville plumbing has its own problems.

Water in Burnsville is very hard. According to the City of Burnsville's 2025 report, hardness is 25 to 26 grains per gallon, and that number explains a lot of what we see. Scale coats heating elements, clogs aerators and wears out faucet cartridges well before their time.

A water softener is not a luxury here. One set to the right number protects the heater, the fixtures and the appliances, and our water softener page explains how that works. A tankless heater needs the same protection, which is covered under tankless water heater service.

Winter is the other local factor. Burnsville sits in Dakota County, where a hard freeze can split a pipe in an exterior wall or an unheated space. When that happens, burst pipe repair is a same-day call. A leak that is slow rather than sudden is a job for leak detection, which starts with a look at the water meter.

Drains and sewers.

Older drain lines show their age through roots, scale and offset joints. A single slow sink is one thing. Several fixtures slowing down together points to the main line, and that is when drain cleaning and a camera inspection come in.

If water or sewage is coming up in a basement drain, stop using water and call. The City of Burnsville asks residents with a sewer backup to call Public Works at 952-895-4550 on weekdays and 9-1-1 evenings and weekends. If the blockage is in your own line, we clear it with sewer line cleaning.

Basements and sump pumps.

A wet basement in spring is common in the Twin Cities suburbs. A sump pump that has sat idle all winter often fails on its first real test. Our sump pump page covers testing, check valves and battery backups, and basement waterproofing explains what a plumber can and cannot fix.

Hot water.

A cold shower is the most common reason people call us after hours. We repair gas, electric and tankless units, and we replace tanks that have failed. Start with water heater repair if the unit still holds water, or water heater replacement if the tank leaks.
