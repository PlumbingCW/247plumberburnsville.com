Boiler Repair in Burnsville, MN.

Hot water boilers heat some Burnsville homes and buildings, and a no-heat day in a Minnesota winter cannot wait. We diagnose and repair residential boilers and answer calls around the clock.

A boiler heats water and sends it through pipes to radiators, baseboards or in-floor loops. When one fails in January, the house cools quickly.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility from outside. For a no-heat call without a gas smell, call us. We answer 24/7.

Boilers are less common than forced-air furnaces in the Twin Cities suburbs, but they are common in older homes, townhomes and some multifamily and commercial buildings. They are plumbing systems as well as heating systems, and our plumbers work on the water side.

Safety first before the plumber arrives.

What to do before we arrive.

  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility and 9-1-1 from outside..
  • If a carbon monoxide detector sounds, get everyone outside and call 9-1-1..
  • Do not relight a boiler that smells of gas or shows soot..
  • If water is leaking from the boiler, switch it off and close the water supply valve to it..

No-heat calls are answered 24/7. A gas smell or a carbon monoxide alarm is an emergency for the utility and 9-1-1 first, and for us second.

Hard water and boilers.

The City of Burnsville's 2025 report lists tap water hardness at 25 to 26 grains per gallon. A closed-loop boiler recirculates the same water and does not scale much once it is filled. The makeup water that enters when the system loses pressure is what adds minerals, so a system that leaks and refills often gathers scale faster.

That makes a leak more than a nuisance. It is worth tracing and fixing. Our leak detection page covers the tools we use, and a water softener on the makeup line is one way to protect a boiler that needs frequent refilling.

Safety and maintenance.

Boilers burn fuel and need clean combustion, proper venting and a safe pressure relief valve. A carbon monoxide detector on every level is a basic precaution. If it alarms, leave the home and call 9-1-1.

A yearly service visit checks the burner, flushes sediment and tests the safety controls. A system that is serviced before winter is less likely to fail on the coldest night. If your boiler is older and repairs are piling up, we will tell you when replacement makes more sense.

Prepare for a boiler visit.

  • Know where the boiler, shutoff and gas valve are.
  • Write down the model number from the data plate.
  • Note any error codes or lights on the control.
  • Check that the thermostat is set to heat and has power.
  • Keep the area clear so the plumber can reach the unit.

Boilers feed hot water for heat, and many homes use the same boiler or a separate water heater for domestic hot water. We help sort out which system is failing.

Which standards apply to a boiler.

Boiler venting and gas piping follow the manufacturer's instructions and the standards the state has adopted, which are not all in the plumbing code. The water side, including valves, relief piping and make-up water, falls under Minnesota Rules chapter 4714. We tell you which standard applies to each part of the job.
