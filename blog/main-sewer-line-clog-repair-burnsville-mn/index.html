Main Sewer Line Clog Repair in Burnsville, MN.

When more than one drain backs up, the problem is usually the main line. This guide covers the signs, the first steps and what a plumber does.

By the Burnsville 24/7 Plumbing team | Published November 16, 2026
.

Signs of a main line clog.

Multiple fixtures slow.

A tub, sink and toilet all drain badly at once.

Floor drain bubbles.

The basement drain is the lowest opening and shows trouble first.

Gurgling toilets.

Air pushes back through traps as the line fills.

Water in the tub after flushing.

The sewer is full and water finds the lowest fixture.

First steps.

  • Stop using water in the house, including the washer and dishwasher..
  • Do not run chemical drain cleaner in a fully blocked line..
  • Keep people and pets away from any sewage that comes up..
  • Call a plumber or the city, depending on where the blockage is..

A main line backup is an emergency. Our line is answered 24/7.

Where is the blockage?.

A clog inside your own pipes is a job for a plumber. A clog in the public main is a job for the city. The way to tell is a cleanout and a camera. If the line is clear to the property line, the blockage is beyond it.

Find your cleanout, usually a capped pipe in the basement or outside near the foundation. A plumber opens it and checks whether water is backed up inside.

How a plumber clears it.

Open the cleanout.

A plumber confirms whether the line is full.

Cable or jet.

A cable cuts roots and a jetter flushes grease and scale.

Camera.

A camera shows what caused the blockage and the condition of the pipe.

Advise.

You learn whether the clog was a one-time event or a sign of damage.

See sewer line cleaning for how the work is done, hydro jetting for heavy buildup and sewer camera inspection for what the camera shows.

Common causes.

Cause.Clue.Fix.
Tree roots.Repeated clogs, older clay or cast iron.Cutting, then repair if the pipe is broken.
Grease buildup.Slow kitchen drain, then main.Jetting and habits.
Flushed wipes.Sudden clog, often near a bend.Cable and removal.
Bellied or broken pipe.Clogs that return within weeks.Camera and repair.

When cleaning is not enough.

A clog that returns means a pipe condition, not bad luck. A camera shows offset joints, cracks or a low spot where waste settles. Fixing the pipe is the only lasting answer. See sewer line repair for the options.

What you can do to prevent the next one.

Keep grease out of the kitchen drain. Pour it into a can and throw it in the trash when it hardens. Use a hair catcher in showers and tubs. Never flush wipes, and treat the toilet as a drain for paper and waste only.

If the lateral is old and roots are the cause, a yearly cleaning keeps it open, but a repair removes the cause. Ask for a camera pass after any cleaning so you know what you are dealing with. A line in good shape needs little attention, and a line in poor shape tells you early.

What a cleaning costs you in disruption.

A good cleaning is a visit of a few hours. The plumber opens the cleanout, runs the cable or jetter and pulls the camera back through the line. You are asked to keep water use low during the work and to leave the floor around the cleanout clear.

Expect some mess. Water and debris come out of the opening when the line is cleared, so plumbers lay down covering and use a collection method. Ask beforehand where the equipment will be set up so you can move belongings away from the area.

Roots.

A cable with cutting heads clears them, but they grow back unless the pipe is repaired.

Grease.

Jetting removes it, and a change in habits keeps it from returning.

Foreign objects.

Toys, wipes and cloth are pulled out with a retrieval tool or cable.

Collapsed pipe.

A cable stops short, and the camera shows why. Repair is the next step.

If your cleanout is missing or buried, say so on the call. Adding one is a modest job that makes every future cleaning faster and safer.

Keep a note of every backup, with the date and which fixtures were affected. A pattern of backups every few months tells a plumber far more than a single event, and it helps decide between another cleaning and a repair.

Who to call in Burnsville.

The City of Burnsville's Water and Sewer Maintenance page tells residents with a sewer backup to call Public Works at 952-895-4550 on weekdays and to call 9-1-1 evenings and weekends so crews can be dispatched.

The page does not say who owns the pipe between the house and the main, so ask Public Works before you assume. A plumber can show on camera whether the blockage is inside your line or beyond it.

Minnesota licenses plumbers, and the Department of Labor and Industry offers a Check a License tool to verify one.

Cleanouts and sewer lateral pipe are covered by the state standard, the Minnesota Plumbing Code in Minnesota Rules chapter 4714.

We answer the emergency line 24/7 from Burnsville and send plumbers to Lakeville and Savage as well, so the same advice applies if you live just outside the city. If the job turns out to be bigger than a homeowner fix, call and describe what you see.
