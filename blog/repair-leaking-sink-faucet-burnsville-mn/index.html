How to Repair a Leaking Sink Faucet in Burnsville, MN.

A dripping faucet is one of the most common household plumbing problems. The fix is usually a small part, and the right one depends on how the faucet is built.

By the Burnsville 24/7 Plumbing team | Published November 16, 2026

Start by working out which kind of faucet you have.

There are four common designs, and each one fails in its own way. The repair for a washer faucet is nothing like the repair for a ceramic disc faucet, so identify yours before you take anything apart.

Two handles usually means a compression or cartridge design. A single handle is more likely a cartridge, ball or ceramic disc. Look for a model name on the base or under the sink.

Faucet type.What leaks.Part to replace.
Compression.Drips from the spout when closed.Rubber washer and seat.
Cartridge.Drips or leaks around the handle.Cartridge or O-rings.
Ball.Leaks at the base or spout.Seals and springs, or the whole ball.
Ceramic disc.Drips from the spout.Cylinder or seals.

Before you start.

Shut off the water.

Close the two small valves under the sink by turning them clockwise.

Plug the drain.

A small rag keeps screws and parts from falling in.

Take photos.

Pictures of each step make reassembly easy.

Open the faucet.

Run it to release any pressure and drain the lines.

The repair.

Pry off the decorative cap on the handle, remove the screw and lift the handle. Under it you will find a nut or a retaining clip that holds the cartridge or stem. Remove it with an adjustable wrench.

Pull out the worn part and take it to a plumbing supply store so you can match it exactly. Install the new part, add a little plumber's grease to the O-rings and reassemble in reverse order.

Turn the water back on slowly and check for leaks. If the faucet still drips, the seat inside the body may be pitted. At that point a new faucet may be the practical choice. See faucet repair for how we decide between repair and replacement.

Is it really the faucet?.

A wet cabinet under the sink is often blamed on the faucet, but the real source is frequently a supply line, a shutoff valve or the drain connection. Dry each connection with a paper towel and watch which one gets wet first.

A slow leak that goes unnoticed rots the cabinet floor and can feed mold behind it. If the leak does not follow the faucet, a leak detection visit finds the source.

Wet at a supply line.

A cracked or loose braided line needs replacement.

Wet at the shutoff valve.

An old valve stem packing is failing.

Wet at the drain.

A loose slip nut or a worn washer.

Wet only when running.

The faucet or sprayer hose is leaking.

Low flow and scale.

Aerator screens clog with mineral deposits, and the stream slows or sprays sideways. Unscrew the aerator, soak it in white vinegar and scrub with a toothbrush. In hard water this is a regular chore.

A softener helps a great deal, and you can read about the options on our water softener page.

When to stop and call a plumber.

Faucet repair is a good first plumbing project, but a few signs mean stop. If a shutoff valve under the sink will not turn or leaks when you move it, do not force it. A corroded valve can break and turn a drip into a flood.

If you shut off the water at the main to work, and the main valve itself will not close fully, call for help before you take anything apart. A faucet that has no working shutoff upstream is not a safe project.

Tools and parts for the job.

Most faucet repairs need an adjustable wrench, a screwdriver set, a small hex key set and a flashlight. A cartridge puller makes some brands easier. Plumber's grease keeps new O-rings supple and helps them seal.

Buy the exact replacement part for your faucet brand and model, not a generic one. Take the old part to a plumbing supply store and compare it side by side. A part that is a millimeter off will leak.

Keep the old parts in a bag until the new ones are working. If the repair fails, the old part is the quickest way to prove which one you need.

Burnsville water and faucet wear.

The City of Burnsville's 2025 drinking water report lists hardness at 25 to 26 grains per gallon. Mineral deposits from hard water wear cartridges and seals, clog aerators and stiffen handles.

The city's Water and Sewer Maintenance page notes that a digital meter that moves while no water is in use may point to a leak. If the faucet drips but the meter runs even with every fixture off, the leak is somewhere else too.

If you hire out the work, Minnesota's Department of Labor and Industry offers a Check a License tool for plumbers.

Faucet supply connections and shutoff valves are covered by the state standard in Minnesota Rules chapter 4714.
