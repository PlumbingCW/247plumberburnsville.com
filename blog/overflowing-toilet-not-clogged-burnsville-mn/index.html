Overflowing Toilet That Is Not Clogged in Burnsville, MN.

A toilet that overflows with nothing stuck in it is confusing. The cause is usually further down the pipe than the bowl.

By the Burnsville 24/7 Plumbing team | Published November 16, 2026
.
Plumbing question?

.Call Now.

Stop the water first.

Do this right now.

  • Take the lid off the tank and push the flapper down, or turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise..
  • Do not flush again..
  • Stop running water elsewhere in the house, including the washer and dishwasher..
  • Protect the floor with towels and move rugs away..

If sewage is coming up in a tub or floor drain, stop using all water and call.

A toilet bowl holds only so much. If water rises and spills, something downstream is not letting it drain. The question is whether the problem is in the toilet, in the branch line or in the main sewer.

Likely causes when the bowl is clear.

Cause.Clue.Who fixes it.
Blocked branch or main line.Other fixtures gurgle or drain slowly.A plumber with a cable or jetter.
Blocked vent.Gurgling and slow flush, worse in wet weather.A plumber, who checks the roof vent.
Fill valve stuck open.Tank keeps running and fills too high.Replace the fill valve.
Partial clog deep in the trap.Weak flush, rising water, then slow drain.An auger or toilet removal.
Main sewer problem.Basement drain backs up too.Call a plumber right away.

Tank problems that overflow the bowl.

A fill valve that does not shut off keeps adding water. The tank overflows through the overflow tube into the bowl, and the bowl can overflow onto the floor. Lift the tank lid and look at the water level. It should sit about an inch below the top of the overflow tube.

If the water is at the top and still rising, close the shutoff and replace the fill valve. See toilet repair for how we handle this.

When the problem is the line.

Several fixtures affected.

Tub, sink or floor drain bubbling when the toilet flushes.

Basement floor drain backs up.

This is the lowest opening, so it shows trouble first.

Gurgling sounds.

Air is being pushed back through the traps.

Repeat overflows.

A pattern points to roots, scale or a bellied pipe.

A line problem needs a cable or a jetter and often a camera. See drain cleaning for what clears the blockage, and sewer line cleaning when it is the main. Camera findings are explained on the sewer camera inspection page.

What to do after an overflow.

Clean the floor with a disinfectant if the water came from the toilet bowl. If sewage is involved, wear gloves and dry the area thoroughly. Porous materials such as carpet that stayed wet for a day or more may need to be removed.

Take photos for insurance, and ask whether your policy covers sewer backup, which is often a separate endorsement.

Prevention that actually works.

Most overflows can be avoided with a few habits. Flush only waste and toilet paper. Keep wipes, even those labeled flushable, out of the bowl. Do not use the toilet as a wastebasket for cotton products or paper towels.

Pay attention to early warning signs. A toilet that gurgles when the washer drains, a floor drain that smells or a slow drain on the lowest level all say the main line is working harder than it should. A cleaning scheduled in good weather costs less than an emergency at midnight.

A quick way to tell where the problem is.

Run water in a sink on another floor and watch the toilet. If the toilet bowl rises or gurgles, the branch or main line is restricted. Flush the toilet and watch the tub or floor drain. If water appears there, the main line is almost certainly the problem.

If nothing else reacts, the trouble is probably local to the toilet, either a partial clog in the trap or a tank that overfills. Write down what you saw before you call so the plumber can bring the right tools.

If a plumber has to pull the toilet, ask to see the wax ring and the flange. A flange that is cracked or sits below the floor surface causes leaks and slow flushes, and fixing it while the toilet is off saves a second visit.

What the City of Burnsville asks residents to do.

The City of Burnsville's Water and Sewer Maintenance page tells residents with a sewer backup to call Public Works at 952-895-4550 on weekdays and to call 9-1-1 evenings and weekends so crews can be dispatched. If the blockage is in the city main, the city handles it. If it is in your own line, a plumber does.

The same page notes that a digital water meter that moves while no water is in use may point to a leak. A fill valve that never shuts off is one way a toilet leaks.

Minnesota licenses plumbers. The Department of Labor and Industry's Check a License tool lets you verify one.

Toilet flanges, traps and vents fall under the state standard in Minnesota Rules chapter 4714.

We answer the emergency line 24/7 from Burnsville and send plumbers to Savage and Lakeville as well, so the same advice applies if you live just outside the city. If the job turns out to be bigger than a homeowner fix, call and describe what you see.
