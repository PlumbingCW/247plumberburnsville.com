Replacing Heating Elements in a Water Heater in Burnsville, MN.

An electric water heater has two heating elements, and one failing is the usual reason for lukewarm water. This guide covers the test, the swap and how to make the new ones last.

How the two elements work.

An electric tank has an upper and a lower element. The upper one heats the top of the tank first. Then power shifts to the lower one, which heats the rest.

If only the lower element fails, you get a small supply of hot water that runs out quickly. If the upper one fails, you may get no hot water at all.

This split tells you something before you open anything. Hot but short-lived water points to the lower element. Lukewarm water that never gets hot points to the upper one or a thermostat problem.

Electric safety before you touch the heater.

  • Turn off the breaker for the heater at the panel, not just the thermostat..
  • Test the terminals with a voltage tester to confirm there is no power..
  • Never work on a heater with a wet floor around it..
  • If you are unsure of any step, stop and call a plumber or electrician..

Water and power together are dangerous. Our line is open 24/7 if the tank is leaking.

Testing an element.

Power off.

Turn off the breaker and confirm with a tester.

Open the panel.

Remove the cover and the insulation to reach the element terminals.

Disconnect the wires.

Note where each one goes before you pull them.

Test continuity.

A meter on the terminals should read a low resistance, not open.

Replacing the element.

Drain the tank below the level of the element before you unscrew it. Use an element wrench to back it out. Check the replacement element wattage and style against the data plate. Mismatched wattage can overload the circuit or fail to heat properly.

Install the new element with a fresh gasket and tighten it firmly. Refill the tank completely and open a hot tap to let air escape before you restore power. Powering an element that is not submerged burns it out in seconds.

Why hard water kills elements.

Minerals precipitate onto a hot element and form a crust. The crust insulates the element, so it runs hotter and burns out. A tank with heavy sediment loses elements regularly.

Flushing the tank each year clears sediment before it builds up. A softener reduces the minerals that cause the crust. See water softener installation for the long-term fix.

When replacing the tank makes more sense.

Elements are cheap. A tank that is leaking, heavily rusted or near the end of its normal life is not worth saving. If the tank is more than a decade old, ask whether new elements are the best use of money. See water heater replacement for what a swap involves.

Mistakes with electric heaters.

The costly mistakes happen before the new element goes in. Buying the wrong wattage or the wrong mounting style wastes a trip. Forgetting to drain the tank wastes the afternoon and floods the floor. Powering the heater before the tank is full destroys the new element within seconds.

The simplest way to avoid all three is to photograph the data plate and the old element, take both to the store and write down the steps in order before you start. If any step feels unclear, the cost of a service call is small next to the cost of a repeated mistake.

How to flush the tank while you have it open.

Draining the tank for an element swap is a good moment to flush it. Attach a hose to the drain valve, run the water to a floor drain or outside and open a hot tap upstairs to let air in. Sediment flows out with the water.

If the water keeps running cloudy, open the cold inlet briefly to stir the bottom and drain again. A few minutes of flushing removes loose scale, and a tank that drains well has less sediment to destroy the next element.

Burnsville hardness and your elements.

The City of Burnsville's 2025 drinking water report lists hardness at 25 to 26 grains per gallon. That is very hard water, and it is the reason a tank in Burnsville often needs elements sooner than the same heater in a soft water area.

The City of Burnsville lists water heaters and softeners among the projects handled through its plumbing permit process. Call Building Inspections at 952-895-4444 to confirm whether an element swap or a full replacement needs a permit.

Minnesota's Department of Labor and Industry offers a Check a License tool if you hire a plumber.

Water heater work is held to the state standard, the Minnesota Plumbing Code in Minnesota Rules chapter 4714.
