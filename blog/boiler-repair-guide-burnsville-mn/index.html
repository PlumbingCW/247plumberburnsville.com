Boiler Repair for Burnsville, MN Homes.

A boiler is a closed heating system with a burner, a pump and a set of controls. When one part stops, the whole house cools. This guide walks through the common faults.

What a boiler does.

A boiler heats water and circulates it through radiators, baseboards or floor loops. The water is in a closed loop, so it goes round and round. A burner heats it, a circulator pump moves it and controls tell both when to run.

Because it is a sealed loop, trapped air, low pressure and a failed pump are the usual causes of trouble.

Check these first.

Thermostat.

Confirm it is set to heat and the batteries are fresh.

Power.

Check the breaker and the switch at the boiler.

Pressure gauge.

Most residential boilers run around 12 to 15 psi cold. Check the manual.

Error code.

Read the display and write down the code.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility from outside. Do not try to relight a boiler that smells of gas.

Common faults.

Symptom.Probable cause.What happens next.
No heat, no burner.Ignition or flame sensor fault.A plumber tests the controls.
Burner runs, radiators cold.Air in the loop or failed circulator.Bleed the air, test the pump.
Pressure low.Leak or failed expansion tank.Find the leak and test the tank.
Banging or kettling.Scale or low flow.Flush and check water flow.
Puddle under the boiler.Leaking valve, fitting or tank.Shut it down and call.

Bleeding air from radiators.

Radiators that are cold at the top have air trapped inside. Use a radiator key to open the bleed valve a quarter turn until water spits, then close it. Watch the boiler pressure afterward, since bleeding lowers it.

If the pressure drops too far, the boiler may need to be refilled. Do not overfill. If you must refill often, there is a leak.

Leaks and make-up water.

A boiler that needs regular refilling is losing water somewhere. Each refill brings fresh water with minerals and oxygen, which shortens the life of the system. Find the leak instead of living with it. A leak detection visit helps trace it.

Repair or replace.

Circulators, controls and valves are repairable on a sound boiler. A cracked heat exchanger or a heavily corroded tank is not. If your boiler is old and repairs are mounting, ask for a straight comparison of repair and replacement cost.

See boiler repair for how we handle no-heat calls, and water heater repair when a separate tank supplies the hot water.

Seasonal care that prevents no-heat calls.

Have the boiler serviced before the first cold snap, not during it. A technician checks the burner, tests the safety controls, flushes sediment and inspects the venting. Bleed the radiators at the start of the season and watch the pressure for a few days.

Keep the area around the boiler clear. Stored boxes and paint cans do not belong near a burner. Check the carbon monoxide detectors on every level when you change the clocks, and replace batteries on schedule.

Reading the signs of a failing circulator pump.

The circulator is the small pump that pushes hot water around the loop. When it weakens, the boiler gets hot but the house does not. You may notice that the supply pipe near the boiler is too hot to touch while the return line is cool. A humming pump that does not spin points to a stuck rotor, and a loud grinding sound points to worn bearings.

Pumps are replaceable parts. A plumber isolates the pump with its flange valves, swaps it and bleeds the air that gets into the loop. If the boiler has no isolation valves at the pump, adding them during the repair saves money on every later service.

Information that helps a plumber on the phone.

  • The boiler brand and the model number on the data plate.
  • Whether the burner fires and whether the pump runs.
  • The pressure reading on the gauge.
  • Which radiators or zones are cold.
  • Any error code or blinking light pattern.

What to do if the boiler smells or alarms.

  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility from outside..
  • If a carbon monoxide detector sounds, get everyone outside and call 9-1-1..
  • If water is leaking, switch the boiler off and close its supply valve..

Do these first. Then call a plumber for a boiler problem that is not a gas or carbon monoxide emergency.

A last point on cost and timing. Parts for a boiler repair are often stocked, so a same-visit fix is common. A cracked heat exchanger or a failed control board that must be ordered can mean waiting days, and a space heater plan for the house is worth thinking about while you wait.

Hard water and boiler water in Burnsville.

The City of Burnsville's 2025 drinking water report lists hardness at 25 to 26 grains per gallon. A closed boiler loop does not scale much once filled, but make-up water adds minerals each time the system loses pressure and is refilled.

The City of Burnsville lists water heaters and softeners among permitted plumbing projects. Call Building Inspections at 952-895-4444 to confirm what applies to a boiler replacement.

Minnesota licenses plumbers. Use the Department of Labor and Industry's Check a License tool to verify the person working on your system.

Water-side boiler work follows the Minnesota Plumbing Code, the state standard in Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, while gas piping and venting follow other adopted standards and the maker's instructions.
