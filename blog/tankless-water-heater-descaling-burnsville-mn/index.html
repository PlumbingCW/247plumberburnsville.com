Tankless Water Heater Cleaning Solution in Burnsville, MN.

A tankless heater in hard water is a heat exchanger waiting to scale up. This guide covers the cleaning solution, the flush and the schedule that keeps the unit running.

Why tankless units need cleaning.

A tankless heater passes cold water over a hot metal heat exchanger. Dissolved calcium and magnesium come out of the water when it heats and stick to that metal. The layer is thin at first, then thick enough to restrict flow.

A scaled exchanger heats less efficiently, throws error codes and eventually fails. The cleaning step that removes the scale is called descaling or flushing.

What cleaning solution to use.

Most manufacturers approve a food-grade white vinegar or a purpose-made descaling solution. Vinegar is mild and widely available. Commercial solutions are stronger and are designed to be circulated and rinsed without harming the unit.

Do not use harsh drain cleaners or random acids. They can damage seals and the exchanger itself. Always follow the instructions for your model, because some warranties spell out the approved method.

White vinegar.Cheap, mild, easy to find.Slower on heavy scale, needs more circulation time.

Citric acid solution.Effective and gentle on metals.Needs correct dilution and rinsing.

The flush, step by step.

Shut down.

Turn off power and gas, then close the isolation valves on the water lines.

Connect.

Attach hoses from a small pump to the service ports and place the solution in a bucket.

Circulate.

Run the solution through the unit for the time the maker specifies, often about an hour.

Rinse and restore.

Flush with clean water, clean the inlet filter, reopen the valves and test the unit.

A tankless unit that was installed without isolation valves needs them added first. They are inexpensive and make every future flush simple. If your unit lacks them, ask a plumber to add them during a tankless water heater visit.

How often in hard water.

The harder the water, the more often the flush. At 25 to 26 grains per gallon, once a year is a reasonable starting point and some units benefit from more. A softener set correctly upstream may let you stretch the schedule.

Watch for early signs: lukewarm water at full flow, temperature swings, a louder burner and error codes that point to flow or the heat exchanger. Do not wait for a failure.

Lukewarm water.

Scale on the exchanger lowers heat transfer.

Error codes.

Flow and temperature codes often trace to scale.

Noise.

A louder burner or kettle sounds suggest a restricted exchanger.

Low flow.

A clogged inlet filter is a separate but related cause.

What a softener changes.

A water softener removes much of the hardness before it reaches the heater. That reduces scale and extends the time between flushes. It does not replace the flush entirely, because softeners can fail or be set wrong.

The softener also needs the right setting. Too little and scale gets through. Too much and the water may turn corrosive. A plumber can set it from a tap test.

Mistakes that shorten a tankless heater's life.

The most common mistake is skipping the flush until an error code appears. By then scale has already insulated part of the heat exchanger, and the unit has been running hot for months. A flush on a schedule costs far less than a replacement exchanger.

The second mistake is running the wrong solution through the unit or leaving it in too long. Follow the manufacturer's time and dilution. The third is forgetting the inlet filter, a small screen that catches grit and clogs quietly. Pull it, rinse it and put it back each time you flush.

A simple yearly routine.

  • Shut off power and gas, then close the isolation valves.
  • Circulate the approved solution for the time in the manual.
  • Rinse with clean water until it runs clear.
  • Clean the inlet screen and check for leaks.
  • Write the date on the unit so the next flush is not a guess.

Keep the receipt for the cleaning solution and a note of the date. If a warranty claim comes up, a record of regular maintenance helps your case.

Burnsville water and tankless heaters.

The City of Burnsville's 2025 drinking water report lists hardness at 25 to 26 grains per gallon. That figure is the reason a yearly descale is a sensible default for a tankless heater in the city.

The City of Burnsville lists water heaters and water softeners among permitted plumbing projects, so ask Building Inspections at 952-895-4444 before changing the installation. A routine flush does not change the plumbing.

For heater questions that touch gas lines or venting, use a plumber licensed in Minnesota. The Department of Labor and Industry's Check a License tool lets you verify one.

Isolation valves and heater connections follow the state standard, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714.
