Emergency Hydro Jetting in Burnsville, MN.

Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scour a pipe clean from the inside. When a cable has already failed or a line keeps clogging, it is often the step that finally restores full flow. We dispatch jetters 24/7, including nights and weekends, with same-day service as appointments allow.

A cable machine opens a path through a blockage. A hydro jetter washes the whole inside surface of the pipe, which is why it works on grease, soap scum and sludge that a cable only punches through. If a line has backed up twice this year, or a plumber snaked it and it stopped again within weeks, jetting is usually the right conversation.

Jetting is also available as an emergency service. When a restaurant line, a main drain or a multi-fixture backup has no other answer, we can dispatch a jetter on a same-day basis. The phone is open 24/7, and the first call is where we decide whether the job needs a jetter or something smaller.

Hydro jetting for a backup that will not clear.

A pipe that has been snaked and keeps clogging is telling you the wall is coated. Cable blades cut a hole the width of the cable and leave the rest of the buildup in place. The next bit of grease or paper catches on what remains, and the line closes again.

Before jetting a line we scope it, because pressure should never be aimed at pipe that is cracked, offset or collapsed. Jetting a broken clay joint can wash soil into the line and make a bad pipe worse. The camera tells us whether the plan is a sewer line cleaning, a jetting pass or a repair first.

What hydro jetting clears well.

  • Grease buildup in kitchen and restaurant lines.
  • Soap and mineral scale that narrows older cast iron.
  • Sludge on the bottom of a nearly flat sewer run.
  • Fine root hairs, after a cutting head has opened the line.
  • Paper and wipes lodged on rough joints.

What happens on a jetting visit.

Camera first.

We run a camera down the line to see pipe material, joints, belly spots and roots.

Access.

A clean-out near the blockage gives the jetter a straight path. If there is none, we plan one.

Jet and cut.

A nozzle with rear-facing jets pulls the hose forward while spray scours the wall.

Verify.

A second camera pass shows the finished pipe, and you see the footage.

Where jetting fits in Burnsville homes.

The City of Burnsville was incorporated in 1964, and much of its housing was built in the 1970s. Original sewer laterals from that era may be cast iron or clay, and cast iron develops a rough, scaled interior that holds grease. A jetting pass on a 1970s lateral often reveals more of the pipe condition than the first blockage did.

Restaurants and retail tenants along County Road 42 and around the Burnsville Center area deal with grease from kitchens and food courts. For those properties we pair jetting with the options on our commercial plumbing page, since a recurring schedule costs less than repeat emergency backups.

Hydro jetting is not always the answer.

Sometimes the camera shows a dip in the pipe where water sits, a section pulled apart by tree roots or a collapsed joint. In those cases jetting only delays the repair. We will say so and describe the next step, whether that is a spot repair, a section replacement or the repair options on our sewer line repair page.

Know your pipe first.

Jetting pressure is chosen for the pipe material and condition. The Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, is the state standard and lists the materials allowed for drains, and a camera pass tells us which one is in your ground before a jetter goes in.
