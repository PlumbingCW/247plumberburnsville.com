Toilet overflowing: stop the water first.

Do this while you wait for us.

  • Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down, or reach behind the toilet and turn the shutoff valve clockwise..
  • Do not flush again, since each flush adds water to a bowl that cannot drain..
  • Put towels at the base and move rugs out of the way..
  • If other drains gurgle or back up, the problem is deeper than the toilet. Stop using water and call..

Overflows are dispatched same day, including nights and weekends. When more than one fixture backs up, see emergency drain cleaning.

Most toilet calls fall into three groups: a clog, a leak at the base or tank, and a toilet that runs or refills on its own. Each has a different fix, and most are finished in a single visit.

We start by finding out which fixture is affected and what else in the house is draining slowly. That tells us whether the problem is the toilet or the line behind it.

A running toilet is not harmless.

A flapper that does not seal lets water trickle from the tank into the bowl all day. The City of Burnsville lists toilets among the common sources of household leaks, and a silent leak can raise a water bill without any visible sign. A drop of food coloring in the tank is a quick test: if color shows in the bowl without flushing, the flapper is leaking.

Parts for this repair are inexpensive and it is quick work. If your bill has jumped and the toilets are clean, see leak detection for other places to check.

When the toilet keeps clogging.

One clog is a toilet problem. Repeated clogs in the same toilet, or a clog that appears with a tub or sink slowing down, point to the drain line. Roots, scale and grease build up inside the pipe and narrow it over time.

A camera inspection shows what is in the line before we recommend anything. See sewer camera inspection to understand what the camera finds. If the camera shows buildup, hydro jetting or cleaning will fix it.

What not to flush.

  • Wipes, including ones labeled flushable.
  • Paper towels, tissues and cotton products.
  • Grease, food scraps or cat litter.
  • Anything that does not dissolve quickly in water.

A toilet that has been replaced or is being moved is a plumbing installation job. For simple repair, our price is given before the work starts.

Fixture standards.

Toilets are set and sealed to the Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, which covers flanges, traps and venting. A toilet that keeps leaking at the base often has a flange problem the standard would have caught.
