Tankless Water Heater in Burnsville, MN.

Tankless units heat water on demand and do not run out, but they depend on correct gas sizing, venting and regular descaling. We install, repair and flush them across Burnsville.

A tankless water heater does not store hot water. Cold water passes through a heat exchanger when a tap opens, and the burner fires to heat it on the way through. That gives you hot water as long as the flow continues, and it frees the floor space a tank would take.

The catch in Burnsville is the water itself. The City of Burnsville's 2025 water report lists hardness at 25 to 26 grains per gallon. Hard water leaves scale inside the heat exchanger, and a scaled exchanger loses efficiency, throws error codes and fails early. Flushing and softening are not optional here.

Flush and descale: the section most owners search for.

A descale service connects a pump and a mild acid solution to the unit's service valves and circulates it through the heat exchanger for a set time. The solution dissolves scale, and a fresh flush rinses it out. In hard water areas the service is normally done once a year, and homes with a softener may stretch it, depending on how the softener is set.

Signs your unit needs it: lukewarm water at high flow, fluctuating temperature, error codes that point to the heat exchanger or flow sensor, and a louder burner than before. A clogged inlet filter is a related but separate fix.

Installing a tankless heater.

Check capacity.

We size the unit to the fixtures you run at the same time and confirm the gas line can supply it.

Plan venting.

Tankless units need a rated vent path, and direct vent models use sealed pipes through a wall or roof.

Install and commission.

The unit is mounted, connected and fired, with isolation valves for future service.

Teach the setup.

We show you the flush ports and the filter so maintenance is simple.

If you are comparing options, our water heater installation page covers tank and heat pump alternatives, and water heater replacement explains what to expect when a failed tank is swapped for a like model.

Questions to ask before choosing tankless.

  • How many fixtures run at once in a typical morning?.
  • Is the gas line sized for the larger input, or does it need an upgrade?.
  • Is there a vent route to the outside that meets the manufacturer's rules?.
  • What will the plan be for hard water, a softener, a scale filter or both?.
  • Who will do the annual descale, and is it priced in?.

Repairing a tankless unit.

Error codes are the first clue. Many point to flow, ignition or venting, and a scaled exchanger often triggers them. We read the code history, check the inlet filter, test flow and venting, then flush the unit if scale is the cause. If a part has failed we identify it and quote the repair or the replacement, side by side.

A water softener upstream of the unit does most to prevent repeat scale problems. Our water heater repair page covers failures on tank units.

Venting and gas standards.

Tankless heaters are installed to the manufacturer's instructions and to the Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714. The vent path and the gas line size are the two points most often corrected at inspection.
