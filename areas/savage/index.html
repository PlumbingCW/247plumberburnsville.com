Emergency plumbing in Savage, day or night.

Savage lies west of Burnsville in Scott County, and the two cities share more than a boundary. According to the City of Savage's drinking water report, 71.5% of all the water distributed in Savage is purchased from Burnsville. That makes Savage plumbing and Burnsville plumbing close cousins: many Savage homes run on the same supply that our team works with every day.

Give the dispatcher your street address first, then describe what you see or hear. Flooding, sewage coming up and a cold water heater are all same-day jobs, nights and weekends included. The steps below limit the mess until the plumber pulls up.

Before we arrive in Savage.

  • Close the main water valve if water is spreading, usually near the meter by the laundry room or furnace..
  • Stop using sinks, tubs and the washing machine if drains are backing up..
  • Turn off power to wet areas only if you can do it from dry ground..
  • Move valuables and rugs off wet floors and take photos for insurance..

What the Savage water report means for your plumbing.

The report says Savage also runs seven wells of its own, 152 to 1,029 feet deep, drawing from several aquifers. The water is treated to disinfect, add fluoride, protect lead and copper plumbing from corrosion and remove iron and manganese. The report does not list a hardness figure for Savage, and Burnsville's own 2025 report puts its hardness at 25 to 26 grains per gallon.

In practice that means a Savage house with a purchased-water supply should expect hard water behavior until a home test shows otherwise. Scale in a water heater, chalky faucet aerators and a softener that needs the right settings are the usual consequences. See water heater repair and water softener installation for what that work involves.
