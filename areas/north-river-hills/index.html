Where North River Hills is.

The City of Burnsville lists North River Hills as one of its organized neighborhoods. The city describes it as bounded on the north by the Bloomington city boundary along the Minnesota River bluff, on the east by Highway 77 (Cedar Avenue), on the south by Cliff Road, and on the west by Burnsville Parkway and 12th Avenue.

That description comes from the city, and we use it as given. If your address sits near one of those edges, call and we can talk through the location before anything is scheduled.

Hard water is the plumbing topic that applies here.

The City of Burnsville's 2025 drinking water report lists hardness at 25 to 26 grains per gallon, which is very hard by any common scale. That figure applies across the city's water system, so it applies to North River Hills homes too.

Hard water leaves scale on heating elements, inside tankless heat exchangers and on faucet aerators. A water heater that takes longer to recover, a faucet with a weak spray, or a kettle-like rumble from the tank are all worth a call. None of these symptoms prove a particular problem in your house, but they are the ordinary results of scale.

Slow hot water.

A tank that runs out faster than it used to may have scale on the elements. See water heater repair.

Weak or spitting faucet.

Mineral buildup in the aerator or cartridge is common with hard water. See faucet repair.

Scale on fixtures.

White crust around taps and showerheads points to hard water. A softener may help, see water softener installation.

Tankless unit alerts.

Error codes on a tankless heater often mean it needs descaling. See tankless water heater service.

Housing in the neighborhood.

The City of Burnsville is a mix of older and newer housing. A 2017 city planning presentation put about 56.0% of the city's housing units at 1980 or earlier, and the city does not publish a neighborhood by neighborhood breakdown in the documents we reviewed. We cannot tell you the age of the pipes in a particular North River Hills house, and we do not assume one. Pipe material is best confirmed by looking at the house itself.

The plumbing standard.

Plumbing work in Minnesota follows the Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, and the City of Burnsville handles plumbing permits and inspections. Ask any plumber how the work will meet the code before you agree to it.
