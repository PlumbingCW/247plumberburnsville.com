Where North East Crystal Lake is.

The City of Burnsville lists North East Crystal Lake as an organized neighborhood. The city describes it as bounded on the north by Crystal Lake and Crystal Beach Park, on the east by Chicago Avenue, on the south by Southcross Drive East, and on the west by Lac Lavon Drive.

Crystal Lake is described as the city's major recreation lake, used for boating, fishing and swimming. Living near a lake does not by itself mean a house has water problems, and we do not claim that it does.

Sump pumps and basement moisture.

Many Minnesota basements use a sump pump to move groundwater away from the foundation. Whether yours is needed depends on your lot, your soil and how the house was built, none of which we can judge from a neighborhood name.

A pump that never runs may be fine, and a pump that runs constantly may have a stuck float or a high water table around the house. A plumber can look at the pit, the float, the check valve and the discharge line.

Pump will not start.

A stuck float or a failed motor can stop the pump. See sump pump repair.

A bad check valve or a float problem can keep it cycling.

Housing in the neighborhood.

The City of Burnsville is a mix of older and newer housing. A 2017 city planning presentation put about 56.0% of the city's housing units at 1980 or earlier, and the city does not publish a neighborhood by neighborhood breakdown in the documents we reviewed. We do not state the age or construction of homes around Crystal Lake, because the sources we reviewed do not give it.

Before the next heavy rain.

Check the pit.

Make sure the sump pit is clear of debris and the float moves freely.

Look at the discharge.

Confirm the discharge pipe is open and drains away from the house.

Know your backup.

If the power goes out, a battery backup pump is a common safeguard to ask about.

Never work on a pump in a flooded basement while the power is on. If water is near outlets or the panel, stay out and call a licensed electrician or your utility.

The plumbing standard.

Plumbing work in Minnesota follows the Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, and the City of Burnsville handles plumbing permits and inspections. Ask any plumber how the work will meet the code before you agree to it.
