Hard water is the Lakeville plumbing story.

The City of Lakeville lists its water hardness at 18 grains per gallon, and suggests a household setting of 14 to 16 grains per gallon, warning that over-softened water may become corrosive. That is a specific, useful number, and it shapes most Lakeville plumbing calls: water heater scale, clogged aerators and softeners that are set wrong.

Lakeville sits next door in Dakota County, so the crew that takes a late call is already close. Leaks, backups and a dead tank all go out the same day.

Water heaters in Lakeville.

At 18 grains per gallon, minerals precipitate onto a tank bottom and electric elements. Popping noises, lukewarm water and a short element life are the signs. A yearly flush helps, and so does a softener upstream. If the tank has started to leak, it is time to replace it.

See water heater repair for troubleshooting, and water heater replacement when a tank has failed. Both are same-day jobs in most cases.

Growing neighborhoods, newer pipes, old fixtures.

A Lakeville home can have copper, PEX or older supply lines, depending on when it was built. Each material ages differently, and so do the shutoff valves on it.

Old shutoff valves that have not been turned in years often leak or seize. Replacing them during a repair avoids a second call. For leaks that are hard to locate, leak detection uses meter tests and acoustic gear. For drains that run slowly, drain cleaning is where we begin.
