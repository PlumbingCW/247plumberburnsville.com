Where Early Lake is.

The City of Burnsville lists Early Lake as an organized neighborhood whose focus is the area around the lake. The city describes it as bounded on the north by 143rd Street West, on the east by Burnhaven Drive, on the south by Southcross Drive, and on the west by County Road 5.

Real estate listings spell the lake name as Earley Lake, while the city's neighborhood page uses Early Lake. We use the city's spelling for the neighborhood.

Running toilets and mystery water bills.

A toilet that runs after flushing can waste a lot of water without making an obvious mess. The City of Burnsville's Public Works guidance explains how to check your water meter for a leak, and that is a good first step when a bill rises unexpectedly.

Turn off every fixture, then watch whether the meter dial still moves. If it does, water is going somewhere, and a leak search can narrow where.

Housing in the neighborhood.

The City of Burnsville is a mix of older and newer housing. A 2017 city planning presentation put about 56.0% of the city's housing units at 1980 or earlier, and the city does not publish a neighborhood by neighborhood breakdown in the documents we reviewed. Fixture and valve wear depends on use and age, so we look at the fixture in front of us instead of assuming a problem from the street name.

A simple home leak check.

Dye test the toilet.

Add a few drops of food coloring to the tank and wait ten minutes without flushing. Color in the bowl means the flapper leaks.

Read the meter.

Note the reading, wait an hour with all water off, and read it again.

Call with what you found.

The numbers help us understand whether the leak is inside or outside the house.

The plumbing standard.

Plumbing work in Minnesota follows the Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, and the City of Burnsville handles plumbing permits and inspections. Ask any plumber how the work will meet the code before you agree to it.
