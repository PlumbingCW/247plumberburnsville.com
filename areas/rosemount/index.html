The service line record.

The City of Rosemount's 2025 drinking water report includes a service line inventory dated October 20, 2025. It lists zero lead lines and two galvanized lines requiring replacement. It also lists 721 lines of unknown material and 8,662 non-lead lines.

That is a lot of unknown. If your home is among the unknown lines, a leak or low pressure may point to the line itself, and we can check it. See water line repair and leak detection.

Water source and what it means at home.

The city reports eight wells 400 to 507 feet deep drawing from the Jordan aquifer. The water is treated to disinfect, add fluoride, protect lead and copper plumbing from corrosion and reduce iron. The report gives fluoride at up to 0.84 parts per million.

The report lists no hardness figure for Rosemount. If you are installing a softener, a hardness test at your tap gives the number to use. See water softener installation.

Water heater scale and clogged aerators remain the usual signs of hard water. For water heater problems, see water heater repair.

Sewer and drain problems.

A backup in a Rosemount home is either a local clog or a problem in the sewer line. A camera pass tells which. See sewer camera inspection for what we look for, and drain cleaning for clearing blockages.

When the camera shows damage, sewer line repair is the next step. For the city sewer main, call Rosemount Public Works at (651) 322-2022.

Winter and spring calls in Rosemount.

Cold snaps bring frozen and split pipes, and spring thaws bring sump pump failures. Both are same-day calls, and both are easier to handle if you know where your main shutoff is before the emergency. See burst pipe repair and sump pump repair for what each visit involves.

A pipe that has frozen once will freeze again, so ask the plumber about insulation or rerouting. A sump pump that runs constantly in spring is working hard, and a backup is worth a conversation.
