When plumbing fails in a business, every hour matters. Customers cannot use restrooms, kitchens shut down and tenants call the property manager. We answer the line 24/7 and dispatch commercial emergencies same day, including nights and weekends.

If water is spreading, close the main or the zone valve, keep people away from wet areas and call. If the building has a fire suppression system or a sprinkler line, do not close those valves without consulting the system owner.

Who we work with.

Restaurants and food service.

Grease lines, floor drains, dishwashers and hand sinks that must stay in service.

Retail and offices.

Restroom fixtures, water heaters, drinking fountains and supply lines.

Multifamily and rental property.

Stacks, shared drains and units that need quick turnaround.

Light industrial and shop space.

Commercial buildings also call us for leak detection. A meter that runs with the building shut down, a wet spot in a parking lot or a damp wall in a shared hallway all need the same meter test and acoustic work as a house, on larger lines. See leak detection for how we trace them.

Permits, code and licensing.

Commercial plumbing is held to the Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, and local inspectors review permitted work. Permits for commercial projects are handled with the City of Burnsville's Building Inspections Department at 952-895-4444. Minnesota requires plumbing work to be done by licensed plumbers, and you can verify any plumber with the Department of Labor and Industry's Check a License tool.

We work with tenants and building owners. If you are a tenant, check the lease first. Many leases make the landlord responsible for the building system and the tenant responsible for fixtures inside the unit.

What to have ready when you call.

  • The business name and the address.
  • Where the problem is and what it is affecting.
  • Whether you are the tenant or the owner.
  • Where the main shutoff and any zone valves are located.
  • Hours the space must be open.

New fixtures, tenant build-outs and restroom remodels are plumbing installation projects, and we price them in writing.

Commercial work and the standard.

Commercial plumbing in Minnesota follows the Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, including grease and fixture requirements for food service. Tenants should confirm with the landlord who is responsible for each part.
