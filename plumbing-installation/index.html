Plumbing installation is planned work: adding a bath, moving a kitchen sink, putting a utility sink in the basement or replacing fixtures during a remodel. It begins with a conversation about what you want, where the water and drain lines are and what the Minnesota Plumbing Code requires.

If a fixture is failing and you need it working now, our repair pages are the better fit. This page covers new work.

The code and the permit.

Minnesota plumbing work is governed by the Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, and enforced by local building officials. The City of Burnsville's plumbing permit page lists water heaters and softeners among the projects that need permits, and Building Inspections at 952-895-4444 can confirm the rule for your project.

Minnesota allows plumbing work by licensed plumbers, and homeowners may do work on a home they own and occupy. Anyone hiring a plumber can use the Department of Labor and Industry's Check a License tool. We pull permits and schedule inspections as part of the work.

Basement bathrooms and below-grade drains.

A basement bath often sits below the level of the main sewer, which means waste may need to be pumped up to the line. An ejector pit and pump handle that, and the pit must be sealed and vented. Planning that before the slab is cut avoids surprises.

If the basement has water problems, deal with them first. Our sump pump and basement waterproofing pages cover that work, and a new bathroom is a poor investment in a basement that floods.

Questions to settle before the work starts.

  • Where will the supply and drain lines run, and will walls or ceilings need opening?.
  • Does the existing water heater have the capacity for a new bath?.
  • Is a permit required, and who pulls it?.
  • Are the fixtures on site and compatible with the rough-in?.
  • Who is responsible for patching and finishing after the plumbing?.

Remodels often reveal old pipe that is worth replacing while the walls are open. If so, see repiping. For fixtures that need a repair rather than an install, start at faucet repair or toilet repair.

The code that governs new work.

New plumbing is built to the Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, and permitted work is inspected against it. That standard is why rough-in is inspected before walls are closed.
