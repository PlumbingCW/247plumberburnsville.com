Main sewer cleaning is the job when a blockage sits in the shared line, not a single branch. Signs are a toilet that bubbles when another fixture runs, water in the basement floor drain and a slow tub on the lowest level. These are the problems that cannot wait until Monday, so we dispatch them 24/7 and treat them as same-day jobs.

If the backup has already started, stop using water in the house and keep everyone off the wet area. Sewage is a health hazard, and anything it touches should be treated as contaminated until it has been cleaned and dried.

Safety first before the plumber arrives.

What to do before we arrive.

  • Stop using water in the house, including the washer and dishwasher..
  • Keep people and pets away from any sewage that has come up..
  • Do not pour chemical drain cleaner into a fully blocked line..
  • Wear gloves and wash well if you touch sewage, and take photos before cleanup..

How we clear a main line.

A main line cleaning begins at the cleanout, usually in the basement or at the foundation. We feed a heavy cable machine with a cutting head into the pipe until it passes the blockage. Roots, paper and rag clogs break up under the blades, and the cable is pulled back while water flushes the debris toward the street.

For a line coated in grease, or one that backs up a few weeks after being cleaned, we move to a hydro jetting pass. After cleaning, a camera run confirms there is no dip, offset or break behind the blockage.

Is it your line or the city main?.

A shared sewer main in the street can back up into houses as well. The City of Burnsville asks residents with a sewer backup to call Public Works at 952-895-4550 on weekdays between 7 a.m. and 3:30 p.m., and to call 9-1-1 on evenings and weekends so crews can be dispatched. If several neighbors have the same symptoms, assume the city main.

If only your house is affected, the blockage is almost certainly on your side, and a camera pass will confirm it. That distinction matters for who pays, so we show the footage rather than describe it.

Keep wipes out of the toilet.

Products labeled flushable do not break down like paper. They collect on roots and joint edges and form the core of most paper-and-rag stoppages we clear in older Burnsville lines. A trash can beside the toilet costs less than a main line cleaning.

Cleanouts and the standard.

The state standard, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, expects drain lines to have accessible cleanouts. If yours is missing or buried, say so when you call, because adding one is the simplest improvement to the line.
