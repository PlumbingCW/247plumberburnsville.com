A burst pipe is an emergency: do this first.

Shut the main.

Close the main water valve, usually near the meter by the laundry room or furnace.

Kill the power.

If water is near outlets or the panel, turn off power at the breaker only if you can do it from dry ground.

Open a faucet.

Run a cold tap on the lowest level to drain remaining water and relieve pressure.

Call us.

The line is open 24/7. Tell us where the water is coming from.

Minnesota winters are the reason burst pipes are a regular emergency here. Water expands as it freezes, and the pipe usually fails at the ice blockage, then the break shows up when it thaws. Pipes in exterior walls, unheated garages and crawlspaces are the usual victims.

We dispatch burst pipe calls same day, including nights, weekends and holidays. The plumber's job is to stop the water, find the break, and repair or replace the damaged section. Then we help you decide what else needs checking.

What we do on the visit.

Stop the water.

Close the right valve to isolate the break and keep the rest of the house in service if possible.

Find the break.

We trace the pipe to the burst point and check the rest of the run for damage.

Repair.

The failed section is cut out and replaced with the right material, copper, PEX or other pipe as needed.

Test.

We pressurize the line, check for leaks and document the repair for your insurer.

Frozen pipes and what to do.

If a pipe is frozen but not yet burst, open the faucet it serves, keep the cabinet doors open and apply gentle heat toward the pipe from a hair dryer or space heater placed safely away from water. Never use an open flame. If no water comes out at all and you cannot find the frozen spot, call us before the pipe bursts.

Prevention matters as much as repair. Insulate pipes in exterior walls and unheated areas, let faucets drip during a hard freeze and know where your main shutoff is before you need it. Our leak detection page covers how to read your meter if you suspect a small hidden leak after a freeze.

Pipe bulging or frosted.

A visible bulge or frost on a pipe means ice inside it. Open the tap and warm the area.

No water from one faucet.

A single faucet with no flow suggests a frozen branch line, not a city outage.

Water stain appearing fast.

A stain that grows by the hour indicates a break and a need to shut the main.

Hissing in a wall.

A pressurized leak makes a hissing sound that grows quieter if the main is closed.

Pipe materials we repair in Burnsville homes.

Homes here have copper supply lines, newer PEX or older materials, depending on their age. Copper splits along its length when ice forms inside, and a repair means cutting out the split section and soldering or fitting in a new length. PEX is more freeze-tolerant but can still fail at fittings.

The City of Burnsville's 2025 report shows 17,004 non-lead service lines and none flagged for replacement as lead or galvanized, so the city-side service line is generally not the cause when a pipe inside the house bursts. If the break is between the meter and the home, see water line repair.

After the water is stopped.

  • Photograph the damaged area and any wet items before cleanup begins.
  • Move belongings off the wet floor and run fans or a dehumidifier.
  • Call your homeowners insurer and ask what they need documented.
  • Ask us for a written description of the cause and repair.

A burst pipe often shows weak points elsewhere in the same run. If a house has pipes that keep failing, a longer conversation about repiping may save money compared with repeated repairs.

Repairs are made to code.

A repaired section of supply pipe must use materials and fittings the Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, allows. A temporary clamp is not a standard repair, and we tell you when one is only a stopgap.
