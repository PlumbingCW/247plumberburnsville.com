A jammed disposal is usually a quick fix. A leaking one is more serious, because water under the sink can reach the cabinet floor and the wall behind it. If water is leaking, turn off the power to the disposal at the switch and the breaker, close the sink shutoff valves and call.

We answer the line 24/7. A kitchen with a backed-up sink, a leak or no working disposal is dispatched same day.

Safe first steps for a jammed disposal.

Before you touch anything.

  • Turn off the wall switch and unplug the unit or turn off the breaker..
  • Never put your hand inside the disposal, even when it is off..
  • Use a flashlight to look for a spoon, bone or fruit pit, and use tongs to remove it..
  • Find the hex socket on the bottom of the unit and turn it with an Allen wrench to free the blades, then press the red reset button..

If the unit still hums or the reset trips again, stop and call. Repeated resets can burn out the motor.

A disposal that drains slowly can be a clog past the unit, not the unit itself. Grease, coffee grounds and starchy food collect in the kitchen drain line over time. See drain cleaning for what clears those blockages.

Many kitchens share a drain line with the dishwasher, so a clog can send dirty water into the dishwasher too. We check both when we clear the line.

Repair or replace.

A disposal is a relatively inexpensive appliance, and repair stops making sense when the housing leaks or the motor is burned out. A new unit costs less than repeated service calls on a failing one.

Installing a new disposal where none exists is plumbing installation, and it needs an electrical connection that matches code. We can handle the plumbing side and advise on the electrical.

What not to put down the disposal.

  • Grease, oil and fat.
  • Coffee grounds and eggshells in large amounts.
  • Pasta, rice and potato peels, which swell and clog.
  • Bones, fruit pits and fibrous vegetables such as celery.
  • Non-food items of any kind.

Drain connection standards.

A disposal drain must connect through a trap and follow the Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714. Shared drains with a dishwasher also need a high loop or air gap under the same standard.

If the sink still drains slowly after the disposal is cleared, the blockage may sit further down the line. Sewer line cleaning covers the main drain when a whole-house backup is the real cause.
