Sewer Inspection in Burnsville, MN.

Sewer camera inspection is a planned visit, not an emergency call. A small camera on a push cable travels the line and records pipe condition, joints, roots and sags so repair decisions rest on what is actually underground.

You cannot see a sewer line from the surface. A camera can, and the footage replaces guesswork with a recorded view of the pipe from the cleanout to the city connection. A good inspection takes under an hour for most Burnsville homes and gives you a clear answer to the question of whether the line is sound.

The most common reasons to schedule one are buying or selling a home, repeat slow drains, a sewer smell in the yard and planning a basement bathroom. If water is already coming up through a floor drain, skip this page and call emergency sewer line repair instead.

What the camera shows.

Pipe material.

Cast iron, clay tile, ABS and PVC look different on camera and age differently.

Joints and offsets.

Misaligned couplings catch paper and invite roots.

Root intrusion.

Fine hairlines or thick masses at joints show up as brown mats inside the pipe.

Bellies and sags.

Water standing in a dip signals settled soil or a poor original grade.

Inspection before buying a home.

Burnsville has housing from several eras, and the age of the house tells you what to expect underground. An older home may still have its original lateral line, and a newer one may not. A camera shows what was actually installed in each case. A pre-purchase camera pass costs a small share of the home price and can save a buyer from inheriting a replacement.

Ask the seller for access to the cleanout. If the house has none, we can usually reach the line through a toilet pull or a roof vent, and we will tell you which. Request a copy of the footage and the plumber's written note so you can negotiate repairs using real findings.

What you receive from the visit.

  • A recorded video of the line from entry to the street connection.
  • A plain description of pipe type, condition and any defects.
  • A distance marker for each defect, measured from the cleanout.
  • A recommendation: no action, monitor, clean or repair.

Steps of an inspection visit.

Access.

We locate or create an entry point at a cleanout, vent or fixture.

Record.

The camera runs the full length while a distance counter logs each feature.

Locate.

If a defect is found, a locator marks its position above ground.

Review.

We go through the footage with you and give a written summary.

Inspection costs and when it is worth it.

The price of an inspection depends on access, line length and whether cleaning is needed first. A line with heavy buildup has to be cleared before the camera can pass, and that adds a step. We give you a clear price before work starts. If a problem is found, the same visit usually leads into a plan for repair, and the camera work is not repeated.

If footage shows a line in good shape, you have a record to hold when the house is sold. If it shows damage, you can compare repair options on the sewer line repair page. When the finding is only a blockage, cleaning with hydro jetting may be enough.

What the camera is checked against.

A sewer camera shows pipe condition, not code compliance on its own. We compare what we see to the materials and joint standards in Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, the state plumbing code, and describe the difference in plain words.
